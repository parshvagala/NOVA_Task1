About DJS NOVA

A student-run festival that started with one telescope and turned into two days most of us plan our whole year around.

DJS NOVA began on a hostel rooftop, where a small astronomy club used to meet on clear nights with a single donated telescope and a WhatsApp group of about twenty people. Somewhere between the third and fourth meetup, someone suggested inviting a professor to talk about exoplanets. Forty people showed up. The club realized it had accidentally started something bigger than a hobby.

Now in its third year, DJS NOVA has grown into a two-day festival that mixes the parts of astronomy that usually stay separate: the research talks, the late-night stargazing, and the hands-on engineering that space missions actually run on. It's organized entirely by students, for anyone curious enough to spend a weekend on it.

We keep it free to attend because the only requirement we care about is curiosity, not a budget.

What to expect

The practical details people usually ask us about the week before.

  • Free entry: every talk and the telescope night are open to anyone. Workshops need registration since seats are capped.
  • Bring a laptop: only if you're joining a workshop that says so on the events page.
  • Food is covered: meals and chai are provided across both days, no separate line for it.
  • Weather backup: if the sky clouds over, telescope night moves to a live feed from a clear-sky observatory instead.
  • Getting there: the venue is a five-minute walk from the nearest station, with signage from the main road on both days.
  • Accessibility: all venues used are step-free, and volunteers are briefed to help with anything you need on site.
